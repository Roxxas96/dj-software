# UI: design

Date: 2026-11-08
Status: for the owner's review (builder check done)
Refines: the [Interface section of the main design](2026-10-30-dj-software-design.md#interface). Where this document and that section disagree, this one wins.

## Intent

**Why (owner):** Open Jockey runs from the terminal today. The owner wants to play a set with it
the way they play with VirtualDJ. So the window should look like VirtualDJ, and be set up and
rearranged like it, as closely as possible.

**How the owner plays:** everything is done on the DDJ-SB3. The mouse and keyboard are not
touched during a set. So during a set the screen only displays. The owner looks at:
- **the waveforms, most of the time.** They line up a transition by the bass, mids and highs and
  by the beat ticks. A red tick marks the first beat of each bar (beats 1, 5, 9…).
- **the knobs on screen, sometimes.** They check that the screen matches the controller.
- **the browser,** to find the next track in a playlist or straight from the music folders.
- **the FX slots,** to see which effect is selected.

**Prior art:** VirtualDJ 2026, Pro layout. The owner sent a screenshot and a screen recording of
every layout and customisation option (summarised under "Reference" below; the files are on the
owner's machine, not in the repo).

## Scope: two plans

One design, built as two plans, in this order (owner-approved). Interop and release move down to
plans 11 and 12.

- **Plan 9, the screen you play on.** The owner can play a whole set from the window, with
  nothing in the terminal.
- **Plan 10, making it yours, the VirtualDJ way.** The LAYOUT menu, the waveform display options,
  4-deck view, dragging and folding panels, the pad-mode and FX dropdowns, and the settings
  window.

## Goals: plan 9

1. **One window, Pro layout, dark theme.** Running `open-jockey` with no arguments opens it,
   using `config.toml` for the controller, mapping and audio devices (see "Config" below).
   From top to bottom:
   - the scrolling waveforms;
   - the two deck panels with the mixer between them;
   - the browser.
2. **Scrolling waveforms across the full width.** One lane per deck the SB3 sides show (left
   above right), plus a lane for any other deck still playing, so a deck switched away from
   mid-transition stays visible (as the main design asks). Each lane:
   - **Fixed playhead.** Each waveform scrolls past a playhead in the centre.
   - **Colour by frequency, in one shape** (the owner's VirtualDJ style). Each moment's colour
     comes from its mix of bass, mids and highs: bass-heavy moments warm (red to orange),
     high-heavy moments light (pink to white). The aim is to match VirtualDJ's look; the colours
     are tuned by eye at the owner's hands-on.
   - **Beat ticks** on every beat, and a **red tick** on the first beat of every bar (every 4
     beats from the grid's first downbeat).
   - **Cue markers:** the cue point and the hotcues.
   - **Loops:** the active loop shown as a shaded region.
   - **Default zoom** about the same number of beats as VirtualDJ shows, with a setting
     (`[ui] waveform_beats`) to change it.
   - A deck with no track shows an empty lane.
3. **Two deck panels** (left = deck 1 or 3, right = deck 2 or 4: the deck the SB3 side shows,
   with its letter or number in the side tag). Each shows:
   - the cover art, title and artist;
   - a small overview waveform of the whole track, with the playhead;
   - BPM, key, elapsed and remaining time;
   - the pitch (slider position and percentage);
   - keylock, slip and vinyl states;
   - the three FX slots: each slot's effect name and level, and whether it is on;
   - the pad area: the current pad mode's name and its eight pads (hotcue set or empty, sampler
     slot loaded, and so on). The SB3 does not report its hardware pad mode, so the name shown
     is the last mode button pressed (HOT CUE, FX FADE, SAMPLER, TRANS…) or the software mode
     the app tracks, and a neutral "pads" until a mode button is pressed after launch;
   - the loop size, and IN and OUT;
   - CUE, PLAY and SYNC. SYNC is one-shot in the engine (no lasting sync or tempo master), so
     the panel shows no MASTER or SYNC badge: only states the app really has;
   - a jog wheel that turns with the playhead;
   - the stems status ("stems 40%", "stems").
4. **The mixer** between the decks: for each of the two channels, GAIN, the three EQ knobs, the
   FILTER, the volume fader, the headphone cue button and a level meter, plus the crossfader and
   the master level.
   - **EQ labels follow the knob mode.** In stems mode with the vdj layout the knobs read HIHAT,
     MEL/VOX and KICK; with the raw layout VOCALS, INSTRU and BASS; with the plain EQ HIGH, MID and
     LOW.
5. **Everything follows the SB3 live.** Every knob, fader, button state and pad mode shown on
   screen moves when the controller moves. A control in soft takeover shows where it will pick up
   (a ghost marker), as the main design asks.
6. **The browser, VirtualDJ style:**
   - **Folder tree on the left:**
     - the drives, then their real folders on disk (Macintosh HD, the music drive, then its
       genre folders, as in the owner's VirtualDJ);
     - below them, the app's playlists.
     A folder lists just its own tracks, not one big library.
   - **Track list in the middle:** Title, Artist, Remix, Length, BPM and Key, plus the existing
     "prepared" bullet and "missing" flag.
   - **Info panel on the right:** the highlighted track's cover and details.
   - **Driven by the SB3.**
     - The BROWSE knob moves the highlight.
     - SHIFT + BROWSE press opens or closes the highlighted folder in the tree, as in the
       owner's VirtualDJ.
     - BROWSE press switches between the tree and the list (the list shows the highlighted
       folder or playlist).
     - LOAD loads the track onto the side's deck.
     - The view scrolls to keep the highlight visible.
   - **Unscanned folders.** A folder the library has never scanned still opens. Its audio files
     (that folder only, not its subfolders) are listed at once from their file names and tags,
     and added to the library as tracks, without registering the folder for watching. They are
     analysed in the background with the existing rules (one worker while a deck plays), and
     their BPM and key appear when ready. Highlighting a drive or a folder never starts a scan
     of what is below it.
   - The tree replaces today's flat "Folders" and "All tracks" entries: the library's folders
     appear in their place on disk.
7. **The status the terminal prints today moves into the window:**
   - warnings, such as a load onto a playing deck or a write that failed;
   - analysis progress;
   - stems progress;
   - controller connected or lost.
   A status line in the top bar, like VirtualDJ's "Computing stems deck 1", shows these. The top
   bar also shows the master meter, the CPU load and the clock.

## Goals: plan 10

1. **The LAYOUT menu** in the top bar, like VirtualDJ's:
   - **four layouts:** Starter, Essentials, Pro (plan 9's) and Performance, each showing more or
     fewer controls, following VirtualDJ's four;
   - **Colour Scheme:** dark or light;
   - **Waveform Display:**
     - colour by frequency (plan 9's);
     - one colour per deck;
     - bands stacked in lanes;
     - both decks over each other in one lane;
     - waveforms at the top or in the middle, above the browser;
   - **Jogwheel Display:** a plain wheel, or one showing BPM, pitch and time;
   - **Beat Counter;**
   - **4-Decks:** four deck panels and a 4-channel mixer (the main design's layout B). The engine
     already has four decks.
2. **Rearranging on screen:**
   - drag any divider between panels to resize them;
   - fold away and bring back the side panels (sideview, info);
   - click a time display to cycle elapsed, remaining and total.
   The arrangement is restored at the next launch. A mappable `ui.layout` action switches
   layouts from a controller button, as the main design asks. Named, shareable layout presets
   are left for later, with skins.
3. **Dropdowns:**
   - the pad mode of each deck's pad bank, offering only the modes the app itself switches
     (beat jump, loop roll, slicer and the others it tracks in software). The SB3's own pad
     modes cannot be set from the host, so offering them would make the screen lie;
   - the effect in each FX slot, per deck as in VirtualDJ. Today `fx.slots` gives every deck
     the same three slots; plan 10 makes them per deck (an engine and config change).
   They change the same state the SB3 changes.
4. **The settings window:**
   - **a sidebar:**
     - Audio (devices and routing);
     - Interface (the layouts as picture tiles);
     - Controllers (controller and mapping);
     - Options;
     - a stub for later sections;
   - **Options:** one searchable list of every `config.toml` setting, with "Show modified" and
     "Reset all", as in VirtualDJ. It includes `[stems] eq_mode`, `knobs` and `kicks_hz`, which
     the stems design said would come to the UI. Changes are written back to `config.toml`.
     Settings that are cheap to apply (display, waveform, key notation, knob layout, FX slots)
     apply at once; the others (audio devices, controller, sample rate, stems split) are marked
     "applies at next launch".
5. **VirtualDJ extras the owner didn't mention.** Each panel the app has something for can be
   turned on or off from the LAYOUT menu or Options:
   - the sideview, for the sampler bank;
   - the info panel;
   - the overview waveforms;
   - the beat counter.

## Non-goals

- **Features the app doesn't have yet:** recording, broadcast, automix, video, karaoke, lyrics
  on the waveform, remixes and the pads editor. The layout leaves room for them, but neither plan
  builds them.
- **Mouse-driven DJing.** No plan here makes the window a way to play (clicking PLAY, dragging the
  jog). Plan 10's dropdowns and settings are for setting up, not performing. (Clickable
  transport may come later; it is not asked for.)
- **MIDI learn and the MIDI monitor in the window.** The main design wants them; they come in a
  later plan, not 9 or 10.
- **Full skins** (user-made skin files, as VirtualDJ's "Edit this skin"). The main design says
  these come later.
- **Two audio devices at once** (master on the MacBook speakers, headphones on the SB3). The
  owner's practice setup needs it, but it is audio work, not screen work: it gets its own plan
  later (owner). Plans 9 and 10 use one audio device, as `play` does today.
- **Copying VirtualDJ's artwork or branding.** The layout, workflow, terminology and dark style
  match VirtualDJ; the logo, icons, skin images and fonts are Open Jockey's own. This keeps the
  project safe to publish. (My call.)

## Anti-goals

The main design's anti-goals stand: no freeze, crash or dropout; a control does only what its
mapping says; cues never disappear. The owner added:

- **The waveforms must never stutter or run behind the sound.** A transition is lined up on
  them. They scroll smoothly at the display's refresh rate, and the playhead shows what is heard
  (allowing for the output latency the engine already knows).
- **The screen must never show a knob, fader, state or mode that differs from the SB3.** It is
  checked against the controller, so a wrong display is worse than none.

And, from the architecture:
- **Drawing can never slow down or interrupt playback.** The UI reads the engine's published
  snapshots and never holds a lock the audio thread waits on. A slow frame skips a frame; it
  never delays audio. The control loop (MIDI to engine) must not wait on the UI either.

## Constraints

- **Rust, egui (via `eframe`)**, as the main design chose (my call there). `egui_dock` or an
  equivalent for plan 10's resizable, foldable panels. The license rules stand (Apache-2.0, no
  GPL, `cargo deny`).
- **Platforms:** macOS, Windows and Linux build and run. The owner plays on a MacBook: plan 9 is
  built for a MacBook screen (Retina, 14 to 16 inch) and scales to larger ones.
- **Startup:** `open-jockey` with no arguments opens the window. A missing controller or audio
  device does not stop it: the window opens, the status line says what is missing, and it
  connects when the device appears (the controller already reconnects this way). The terminal commands stay:
  - `play`, including `play --library`, for headless use and debugging;
  - `library`, `stems` and `midi`.
- **Config:**
  - The window takes the controller, mapping and audio devices from `config.toml`. They are new
    keys where none exist today; `play`'s flags stay as they are.
  - Window state (layout, sizes, folded panels) is saved in its own file in the config folder,
    not in `config.toml`. The main design asks for layout presets as readable TOML.
  - New keys follow the plan 5 rules: `<file>: <key>: <problem>` errors and documentation in
    `docs/config.md`.
- **Library:**
  - The folder tree reads the real disk.
  - Listing a big folder must not block the window. Folder reads and tag reads run off the UI
    thread, as the library worker does today.
  - The existing BROWSE behaviour (tree ⇄ list, LOAD) carries over.
- **Docs and tests change with the behaviour:** the README, `docs/library.md`, `docs/config.md`
  and the SB3 README.
  - UI logic that can be tested without a screen (what a widget shows for a given snapshot, the
    tick positions, the colour function, the browser model) has unit tests.
  - The look is checked by the owner by hand.

## Decided

**By the owner:**
- The window should look and be configured like VirtualDJ, as closely as possible.
- During a set, everything is done on the SB3. The window only displays.
- The default waveform style is colour by frequency in one shape. The lane style (and the
  others) are selectable.
- A red tick marks the first beat of every bar.
- The browser is folder based, like VirtualDJ, not one big library.
- The VirtualDJ extras the owner didn't mention are included as panels that can be turned on or
  off, limited to features the app has.
- Things that would make the owner wince: the waveforms stuttering or lagging, and the screen not
  matching the SB3.
- The split into plan 9 (the screen you play on) and plan 10 (customisation), in that order.
- One audio device for now; two devices come later in their own plan.
- SHIFT + BROWSE press opens and closes tree folders; BROWSE press switches to the list.

**My calls (the owner may still change them):**
- egui, as in the main design.
- Open Jockey's own artwork, not VirtualDJ's.
- `open-jockey` with no arguments opens the window; the terminal modes stay.
- Unscanned folders are added and analysed in the background when browsed.
- Playlists sit below the drives in the tree.
- VirtualDJ's frequency colours, matched by eye at the hands-on.
- The default zoom matches VirtualDJ's, configurable.
- Window state lives in its own file, not `config.toml`.
- Browsing an unscanned folder adds its own files to the library (one level, folder not
  registered for watching); highlighting never starts a scan.
- The tree replaces the flat "Folders" and "All tracks" entries.
- No MASTER or SYNC badge, since sync is one-shot in the engine.
- The pad mode name is the last mode button pressed, "pads" until then.
- Waveform lanes: the decks both sides show, plus any other deck still playing.
- Plan 10's pad-mode dropdown offers only the software modes; FX slots become per deck.
- A `ui.layout` action; named layout presets later, with skins.
- Cheap settings apply at once; device, controller and engine-structure settings at next
  launch.
- A missing controller or device never stops the window from opening.
- The owner's console commands (beatgrid edits, `load`) are not given a window equivalent in
  plan 9. `play --library` keeps them.

## Left to the builder

- **How the waveforms are drawn** (egui meshes or a custom wgpu paint callback), as long as two
  waveforms (four in plan 10) stay smooth at the display's refresh rate on the owner's MacBook.
- **The waveform data.**
  - The library stores three bands at 1024 frames per bucket (about 23 ms at 44.1 kHz).
  - If that is too coarse at the default zoom, the builder may compute finer data from the
    decoded track the deck already holds, off the UI and audio threads.
- **How the UI gets state:**
  - from the engine snapshots, the controller and actions state (pad modes, the SB3 side's deck)
    and the library worker;
  - with one data path per source, without locks on the audio side.
- **How the control loop and the window share the process** (threads, channels), within the
  anti-goals.
- **The exact frequency-to-colour function.**
- **The window state file's format and name.**
- **Which plan 9 widgets are drawn by hand and which come from egui.**
- **How plan 10's four layouts differ in detail,** taking VirtualDJ's as the guide:
  - **Starter:** big jogs, few controls.
  - **Essentials:** pads and FX knobs.
  - **Pro:** everything.
  - **Performance:** compact decks, a bigger browser.
